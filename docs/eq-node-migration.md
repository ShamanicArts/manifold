# Three-band EQNode migration boundary

The original `dsp/core/nodes/EQNode.cpp` is a separate primitive from `EQ8Node`. Rust graph kind 45 implements its serial low shelf, mid peaking filter, and high shelf with two independent channel states. Parameters 0–8 are low gain (`−24…24 dB`), low frequency (`20…400 Hz`), mid gain (`−24…24 dB`), mid frequency (`120…8000 Hz`), mid Q (`0.2…12`), high gain (`−24…24 dB`), high frequency (`2000…16000 Hz`), output (`−24…24 dB`), and wet mix (`0…1`). All nine smooth per sample over 10 ms. Coefficients refresh only when a gain differs by more than `0.02 dB`, a frequency by more than `0.5 Hz`, or Q by more than `0.01`, matching the old node. When target and current mix are both at most `0.0001`, processing copies dry stereo input without advancing filter state.

The [workbench](../projects/eq-node/project.json) exposes all nine physical controls and eight checked-in C++ captures. They cover flat output, individual bands, combined bands, wet mix, output trim, and 64-frame blocks. All eight show **Match** against Rust/Wasm; the largest maximum sample difference is `6.22e-5`. A stronger combined 64-frame sweep previously measured `3.51e-4` maximum difference and was above the workbench's match threshold. The direct-form biquads are numerically sensitive at low frequencies, so this is a known limit of sample-by-sample C++/Wasm comparison rather than a claim of bit identity.

Standalone FX type 14 maps normalized `p/0`, `p/1`, and `p/2` to low gain `−12…12 dB`, high gain `−12…12 dB`, and mid gain `−6…6 dB`. The old Lua slot fixes low frequency at `120 Hz`, mid frequency at `900 Hz`, mid Q at `0.8`, high frequency at `8000 Hz`, output at `0 dB`, and internal mix fully wet. `p/3` and `p/4` are unused. Four new slot cases compare native Rust with Rust/Wasm. Old project-level routing and preset roundtrips remain separate work.
